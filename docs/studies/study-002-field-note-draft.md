# A smaller trading bill is only part of the story

**Unpublished Field Notes draft — separate editorial approval required.**
Prepared from the verified Study 002 report. This file is outside the production
content loader and does not alter the seven existing editorial approval hashes.

Our first 2024 study left a conspicuous bill. The Ridge strategy bought and sold
Bitcoin 570 times in each direction and Ether 499 times. Attributed costs reached
9,932.12 USDT and 9,239.49 USDT against separate 10,000-USDT accounts.

That made a slower rule a reasonable next question. Once a day, compare the latest
completed price with its price exactly 30 days earlier. If it is higher, hold the
asset; otherwise hold cash. Keep the rule fixed and see what happens after costs.

The trading bill became much smaller. The new strategy made 38 executions for
Bitcoin and 32 for Ether. Costs fell to 543.87 USDT and 422.61 USDT, roughly 95%
less than Ridge. Both accounts finished ahead of their starting capital, returning
36.62% and 8.47%.

But the benchmark changed the perspective. Buy & Hold returned 120.81% for Bitcoin
and 46.18% for Ether. The trend rule missed much of those gains. Its Bitcoin
account also experienced a 37.88% observed hourly drawdown, worse than Buy & Hold's
32.31%. Lower costs did not mean smaller losses at every point along the way.

A slow signal can react after a fall and leave before a rebound. In August, the
Bitcoin rule held through the August 5 daily decision, sold on August 6, bought
on August 7, sold on August 8 and bought again on August 9. Those hypothetical
fills occurred at 00:02 UTC. On August 8, Buy & Hold gained 11.88%; Trend returned
−0.05%. A 30-day lookback still crossed its threshold on consecutive days.

The comparison does not tell us that lower frequency caused the improved annual
return over Ridge. The signal changed, the time invested changed and the trades
occurred at different prices. Removing the recorded costs from the final number
would not reconstruct a valid zero-cost portfolio: quantities and the subsequent
capital path would also change.

The useful finding is narrower: this one predeclared slower rule traded much less,
paid much less, and produced different returns and drawdowns on the same inspected
year. It still lost substantial value during some periods and lagged Buy & Hold.
We did not try several lookbacks and select the winner.

These are retrospective historical simulations. We had already examined 2024.
Assumed availability, archive openings and fixed costs do not prove actual fills,
and two correlated markets do not establish a durable edge. Any prospective or
uninspected evaluation needs its own frozen protocol.
